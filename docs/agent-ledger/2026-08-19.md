### The answer: BY DESIGN, and it is the SAME mechanism — not a different hole

The ticket asked whether the training table's missing 2024 is the same mechanism as the runtime
output's deliberate 2024 withholding, or a separate gap. **Same mechanism.**

`scripts/assemble_engine_b_dataset.py:230` delegates to `build_engine_b_features`, which calls
`apply_inference_partition` (`src/dynasty_genius/features/feature_assembly.py:46`, invoked at :325).
That function sorts every season into exactly three buckets:

| bucket | rule | kept? | outcome | `training_eligible` |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| training | `feature_season < inference_season - 1` | yes | present | True |
| **in-between** | no complete 2-year window, not inference | **DROPPED** | — | — |
| inference | `feature_season == max(seasons_window)` | yes | null | False |

`SEASONS = range(2018, 2026)` → `inference_season_rule` returns **2025** → training-eligible is
`feature_season < 2024`. **2024 is therefore neither trainable (2024+2 = 2026 > 2025, window
incomplete) nor the inference season. It is the in-between season, and it is dropped by design.**

### Consequence the ticket did not draw

The ticket says a proposal to make 2024 eligible for a one-year horizon "is unsatisfiable against
the current table." **More precisely: it is not a data problem at all.** 2024's features exist; what
is missing is a *complete 2-year outcome window*, because `OUTCOME_COLUMN = avg_ppg_t1_t2` averages
t+1 and t+2. Making 2024 eligible requires changing the **outcome horizon** — a model-definition
change subject to `00` and David's word — not backfilling data. Nothing needs ingesting.
